01 / The Big Picture

Great content that deserves a better home.

Your website is the official voice of the Egyptian state, and it already speaks three languages. That is a strong start. Right now, though, the experience around that content does not quite match the importance of the institution. The plan below fixes that, step by step.

What is already good

You have the hard part

Real, valuable content: the President's speeches, official news, national initiatives, and the history of the presidential palaces. Three languages. Strong security. We are not starting from zero, we are polishing something that already matters.

The main problem

A few important pages do not work

Some key pages open empty, the same content is loaded twice behind the scenes (which slows things down), and the English and French pages have web addresses written in Arabic letters. Visitors notice these things.

The other problem

It could be easier for everyone

The menus are hard for screen readers to follow, there is no search box, and some text is hard to read. These are exactly the areas a national website should get right for every visitor.

Our recommendation, in one breath

Fix the pages that are letting visitors down today, give the site a cleaner and more elegant look, make it easy to find things in all three languages, and then add a few thoughtful features. We suggest doing it in three simple stages so you see real improvements within weeks, not months.

  1. Fix what is broken. Make the empty pages work, tidy up behind the scenes, and clean up the small things that make a site feel neglected.
  2. Give it a beautiful, easy-to-use design. A dignified light design in red, white, black, and gold, with clear menus and a proper search.
  3. Add helpful new features. A modern news center, a searchable speech library, and a way for people to follow updates.
1

Some important pages open empty

The Speeches page, the "President Answers" page, and the National Projects page showed only a title and no content when we opened them. The National Projects page even has a menu of 21 topics, but choosing one says "No projects found." Visitors who land there may see nothing at all.

2

Every page loads its content twice

Behind the scenes, the full text of each page is included two times. This makes pages heavier and slower, and it confuses the tools that read pages aloud for people with low vision.

3

English and French addresses are written in Arabic

An English news article has a web address made of Arabic letters and symbols. It is hard to read, hard to share, and it makes the English and French pages harder to find on Google.

4

The menu confuses screen readers

Menu items like "Egypt," "Presidency," and "News" are labeled as page titles instead of menu links. For someone using a screen reader, this makes the site much harder to navigate.

5

The three languages are not in sync, and some pages feel old

The links, social icons, and even menu names are different in each language. Some featured reports are from 2021, and the small copyright line at the bottom still says 2020. Little details like these make an official site feel unattended.

03 / What We Found

The details, explained simply.

Here is what we noticed, area by area. The encouraging part: most of these are template-level issues, so fixing them once fixes them across the whole site.

Finding your way aroundHard to explore

The most important content, who the President is, the former presidents, the official reports, sits inside a "Presidency" menu that the English home page barely shows. It is there, but buried.

  • The menus have different names in each language. "News" in English is "Presidential Activities" in Arabic. It can feel like three different websites.
  • The site is built like a rolling news feed, with no easy starting point for citizens looking for initiatives or journalists looking for speeches and photos.
  • There is no search box and no "you are here" trail on deeper pages, so it is easy to get lost.
The look and feelFeels generic

Today the design looks like a standard portal template rather than a carefully crafted national identity. The bright red is used heavily, and the spacing and typography feel like defaults.

  • The same news items repeat several times on the home page, which scatters attention instead of guiding it.
  • Some text, white on bright red and light gray helper text, is likely hard to read for many people.
  • There is no consistent, dignified visual style that instantly says "this is the office of the head of state."
The content itselfStrong, but uneven

The core content is genuinely good, full speeches and beautifully detailed palace histories. The problem is around the edges.

  • Some flagship pages are thin. The Decent Life initiative, a major national program, is just two short paragraphs.
  • A few key pages open empty, as noted above.
  • Old dates (2021 reports, a 2020 footer) make the site feel out of date, even though a lot of the news is current.
Getting things doneSome dead ends

When we tried the things a real visitor would do, a few led nowhere.

  • "Read the latest speech" works, but "browse older speeches" does not, because that page is empty.
  • "Find a project by topic" fails on the very page built for it.
  • There is no clear way to contact the Presidency or sign up for updates. The RSS feeds exist, which is great, but they are hidden away.
  • One nice touch that already works: you can share and copy the link to any article.
Welcoming to everyoneBelow the standard

Government websites should be usable by people with disabilities, and here there is real room to improve.

  • Because content loads twice, screen readers announce everything twice, and keyboard users tab through everything twice.
  • The menu is not built as a proper menu, so it is confusing to navigate without a mouse.
  • There is no "skip to main content" shortcut and no working search, both of which help everyone.
  • Some color combinations are likely too low-contrast to read comfortably.
Three languages, done rightOut of sync

Arabic is the main language and reads right-to-left, which is correct and good. The three versions just need to line up.

  • The footer links and social icons are different in each language.
  • The web addresses are in Arabic even on the English and French pages.
  • The French version is thinner and a little more out of date than the Arabic one.
Speed and search visibilityWeighed down

Because every page loads its content twice, pages are heavier and slower than they need to be, and search engines can see it as duplicated content.

  • Some pages rely on extra code to fill in their content, and when that does not run, the page looks empty.
  • The Arabic web addresses on English and French pages also hold those pages back on Google.
Trust and securityA real strength

This is worth celebrating. The site is well protected, with strong, modern security settings that many organizations get wrong. That is a solid foundation to build on.

  • To round it out, we would add a published privacy statement, an accessibility statement, and a clear "last updated" date on content, the trust signals people expect from an official source.
04 / The New Look

Light, elegant, and unmistakably Egyptian.

We propose a clean, luxurious design on a light canvas, built around your national colors, red, white, black, and gold, inspired by the world's most respected government websites. Calm, confident, and beautiful.

When someone opens the site, it should feel as important and trustworthy as the office it represents. Plenty of white space, a confident solid red, warm gold for moments of ceremony, and elegant type that lets the content breathe.

The colors
  • White as the calm, spacious base, clean and timeless.
  • Red as the confident brand color, refined and used with purpose.
  • Gold for elegance and ceremony, used sparingly so it always feels special, with black for crisp, readable text.
The typography
  • An elegant serif for headlines, the kind of typeface that feels official and refined.
  • A clear, modern, highly readable typeface for everything else, in all three languages.
  • Comfortably large text and generous spacing, especially for long speeches.
The feel
  • Solid color blocks and clean lines instead of clutter, a real sense of order and calm.
  • Gentle, tasteful motion: content fades in softly as you scroll. Elegance, not spectacle.
  • Everything stays fast, and calm for anyone who prefers less movement.

A consistent design kit

Rather than a one-off theme, we build a reusable design kit: a defined set of colors, fonts, buttons, cards, and page layouts that all work together. This keeps every page polished and on-brand, makes future updates easy for your team, and lets the site grow gracefully over time without losing its identity.

05 / Modern Government UI/UX

What makes a government website feel modern.

The best national websites in the world share a few habits. They are calm, clear, and built around people. Here is how we would bring those same habits to the Presidency site.

A strong, steady header

One header on every page with the national emblem, a clear menu, an always-visible search box, and the language switch, so people never feel lost and always know they are on the official site.

A homepage built around tasks

Lead with what visitors came to do, read the latest speech, follow a national project, reach the Presidency, instead of a rotating slideshow. Clear doorways, not decoration.

One consistent component set

Buttons, cards, tabs, and forms that look and behave the same on every page. Consistency is what makes a site feel considered and trustworthy.

Room to read

Generous white space, large type, and comfortable line lengths, especially for long speeches. Content should feel calm and effortless to read on any screen.

Accessible for every citizen

Full keyboard use, clear focus outlines, strong contrast, a "skip to content" link, and an accessibility toolbar for text size and high contrast. A national site should welcome everyone.

Beautiful on a phone

Designed for mobile first, so it works perfectly on a phone without needing a separate app. Most visitors arrive on a small screen.

Clear trust signals

Visible "last updated" dates, official badges, a secure lock, and named sources, so every page feels current, verified, and authoritative.

Information you can see

Turn national projects and reports into maps, simple charts, and progress indicators instead of walls of text. Show results at a glance.

Arabic-first, truly trilingual

Designed right-to-left properly from the start, with the same quality and content in Arabic, English, and French.

The standard we design toward

We aim for the level of the world's most respected government platforms: the United Kingdom's GOV.UK for clarity, the U.S. Web Design System for a documented component kit, Singapore's national portal for calm simplicity, and the French and UAE platforms for elegant multilingual design. The goal is not to copy them, but to give Egypt a website that belongs in their company.

07 / Speaking Every Language Well

An English page should have an English address.

This is one of the easiest wins with the biggest payoff, and it makes the site friendlier to share and easier to find.

Readable web addresses

  • Give each language its own clear address, so an English article reads in English and a French article reads in French.
  • Tell search engines which page is which language, so the right version shows up for the right person.
  • Automatically send old links to the new ones, so nothing breaks.

Three languages that match

  • The same pages, links, and footer in Arabic, English, and French.
  • When a translation is still coming, say so clearly instead of quietly showing something different.
  • Tidy up the stretched Arabic text used for decoration, which also helps search and screen readers.

08 / What To Fix First

Quick wins now, bigger improvements next.

You do not have to do everything at once. Some fixes are fast and make an immediate difference. Others take a little longer but set the site up for the future.

Quick wins, in days to weeks
  1. Make the empty pages work so Speeches, President Answers, and National Projects actually show their content. Most important of all.
  2. Stop loading content twice. This instantly makes pages faster and friendlier for screen readers.
  3. Fix the menu so it behaves like a real menu, and add a "skip to content" shortcut.
  4. Freshen up the dates, starting with the 2020 footer and the old reports.
  5. Improve the hard-to-read colors.
  6. Add a search box and show off the RSS feeds you already have.
  7. Make the footer and links the same in all three languages.
  8. Publish a privacy statement and an accessibility statement.
Bigger improvements, in weeks to months
  1. Give every language readable web addresses, with old links redirected automatically.
  2. Reorganize the menus around what visitors actually need, the same way in every language.
  3. Rebuild pages so they load fully and quickly, even before the extra code runs.
  4. Roll out the new design kit across the whole site.
  5. Deepen the initiative and project pages with goals, numbers, and results.
  6. Bring the three languages to full parity, with a simple translation workflow.
  7. Add proper sitemaps so search engines find everything.
09 / New Ideas

Thoughtful features people will actually use.

Once the essentials are in place, these additions turn the site from a news feed into a modern, helpful national platform, all appropriate for an official government site.

A modern news center

One place for all news, easy to filter by topic, date, and language, with official photos and logos ready for journalists to use.

A searchable speech library

Every speech with its full text, official video, a downloadable copy, and a stable link, easy to search by year or topic.

A national projects hub

The 21 topics filled in with real project pages, maps, budgets, timelines, and a simple dashboard showing progress.

A clear way to reach the Presidency

A simple, respectful contact experience, and a more welcoming home for "The President Answers."

Follow the updates

Let people sign up by email, or get a notification, when there is new news, a new speech, or a live event.

An accessibility helper

A small toolbar to make text bigger, switch to high contrast, or read speeches in a calm, distraction-free view.

A rich media gallery

Beautiful, well-organized photo and video collections of state events, palaces, and projects, with captions in all three languages.

A dedicated live page

For major events: a schedule, a countdown, live captions, and the recording afterward.

A transparency center

One current home for achievement and development reports, published on a regular, predictable schedule.

10 / The Plan

Three clear stages, real progress early.

We suggest working in stages so you see visible wins within the first few weeks, while the bigger pieces come together behind the scenes.

Stage 1
Weeks 1 – 4

Fix what is broken

Make the empty pages work, stop loading content twice, fix the menu and add a "skip to content" shortcut, freshen up the dates, improve the hard-to-read colors, add search, tidy the footer, and publish a privacy and accessibility statement.

The result: the site stops letting visitors down, and immediately feels more cared for.
Stage 2
Weeks 5 – 12

A beautiful, solid foundation

Give every language readable web addresses, add proper sitemaps, roll out the new light red, white, black, and gold design kit, and rebuild the busiest pages so they load fully and fast.

The result: a clean, elegant, easy-to-find site in all three languages.
Stage 3
Months 4 – 9

Bring the site to life

Launch the new menu structure, the modern news center, the searchable speech library, and the filled-in projects hub. Then add follow-by-email, the media gallery, the transparency center, and the accessibility helper.

The result: a modern, welcoming national platform people love to use.
