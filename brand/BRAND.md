## 1. Colour

All values below are printed **as text** on the colour plate — they are read off the page,
not sampled from pixels. They were independently confirmed against the vector fills in the
logo artwork (see §3), which resolve to exactly `#0082CA` and `#F6A909 ≈ #F5A800`.

### Primary — PDF p.6 (printed "Page 05"), "PRIMARY COLORS"

| Swatch | Hex | RGB | Role as used in the deck |
|---|---|---|---|
| Charcoal | `#414042` | 65, 64, 66 | Dominant dark ground; every dark page and sidebar; dark text on light pages |
| Light grey | `#D7D7D7` | 215, 215, 215 | Light ground and light-on-dark text |
| Blue | `#0082CA` | 0, 130, 202 | The logo's dominant colour; the identifying brand accent |

### Secondary — PDF p.6 (printed "Page 05"), "SECONDARY COLORS"

| Swatch | Hex | RGB | Role as used in the deck |
|---|---|---|---|
| Amber | `#F5A800` | 245, 168, 0 | The logo's swoosh; a single warm gesture against the blue |
| Taupe | `#B1A795` | 177, 167, 149 | Neutral warm grey; icon variant fills |

**The deck prints no CMYK, Pantone or spot values** — only the five hex values above. That
is the whole documented palette. There is no documented status/semantic palette (success,
warning, error), no documented tint or shade ramp, and no documented gradient specification
beyond the artwork itself.

### Observed, not specified

The deck's own page background is `#393536`, a slightly deeper shade of the `#414042`
primary; light pages sit on `#E9E9E9`, a tint of the `#D7D7D7` primary, and light-on-dark
text is also `#E9E9E9`. This establishes a precedent — inside this identity, tints and
shades of the two neutral primaries are legitimate working colours. The ramp used in the
proposal (§5) is built on that precedent rather than on invented hues.

## 2. Typography — PDF p.8 (printed "Page 07")

The page is headed **"English Font ( Arial )"** and footed **"Arabic Font (Rabar_13)"**,
with two specimens between them:

| Role | Face as specified | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| English (umbrella) | **Arial** | Page heading, "English Font ( Arial )" |
| Headline | **AlWatanHeadlines (Bold)** | Specimen 1, set in bold caps: "TYPOGRAPHIC HIERACHY" |
| Body | **DIN Next LT Arabic (Light)** | Specimen 2, set as a running paragraph |
| Arabic | **Rabar_13** | Page footer |

The hierarchy rule is stated in prose at the top of the page: *"A typographic hierarchy is a
system that uses typography — the size, font and layout of different pieces of text — to
create a hierarchical division…"*. In practice the deck expresses it as **one grotesque in
two weights**: heavy caps for headlines, light for body. There is **no serif anywhere in the
identity**, and no letter-spacing or capitalisation rule is given numerically.

### Substitutions made, and why

None of the three named faces has a free web release, so each is substituted. Every
substitution is listed here and in the hand-off report; nothing was approximated silently.

| Specified | Used on the web | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| AlWatanHeadlines (Bold) | **Archivo** 600/700, falling back to Arial | AlWatanHeadlines is a licensed newspaper family (Al Watan); its Latin is essentially a Helvetica/Arial-metric grotesque. Archivo is an open grotesque in the same lineage, drawn for display sizes, and holds up at the 112 px cover setting where Arial looks thin and generic. Arial — the face the deck actually names — is the immediate fallback. |
| DIN Next LT Arabic (Light) | **Barlow** 300–600, falling back to Arial | DIN Next LT is Linotype-licensed. Barlow is the closest open relative: a low-contrast grotesque on DIN's skeleton, with a light weight that matches the specified Light register. |
| Rabar_13 | **Cairo** 400/600/700 | Rabar_13 is a Kurdish/Arabic display face with no web release. Cairo is a contemporary Arabic sans on a DIN/Frutiger skeleton, so it lands close to the deck's *other* Arabic instruction (DIN Next LT **Arabic**) and pairs correctly with Barlow. This is the one face the proposal already used, so it is unchanged. |

The practical consequence: the proposal's previous serif/sans pairing (Cormorant Garamond +
Inter) has no basis in this identity and is gone. The document now reads as one grotesque
family in two weights, which is what the deck specifies.

## 3. Logo

### Variants — PDF p.3 (printed "Page 02"), "LOGO VARIATIONS"

The page states: *"There will be tow [two] logo variation and it depends in the position and
purpuse to give different effect each time."* The two variations are **GRADIENT** and
**FLAT**, and each is shown twice, once **on dark background** and once **on light
background** — four cells in total.

Within each cell, two colourways are shown side by side and both are marked acceptable:

- the **full-colour** mark — blue `#0082CA` calligraphy with an amber `#F5A800` swoosh;
- a **monochrome** mark — silver/near-white `#E9E9E9` on dark, dark grey on light.

So on a dark ground **both the colour mark and the silver monochrome mark are approved**.

### To do & not to do — PDF p.4 (printed "Page 03")

Two lockup arrangements are ticked, and one is crossed, in all four cells:

- ✔ **Vertical lockup** — emblem above the Arabic name above the Latin name.
- ✔ **Horizontal lockup** — emblem at left, Arabic name over Latin name at right.
- ✘ **The third row is forbidden.** It shows the horizontal lockup with the *name text
  recoloured* — the wordmark set in blue against a mark whose emblem is monochrome, and the
  mirror-image mistake. The rule this expresses: **do not mix colourways within one lockup.**
  Emblem and wordmark must both be colour, or both be monochrome.

### Placement — PDF p.5 (printed "Page 04")

Verbatim: *"Your logo should be in the upper left corner of websites, social media, and
anything with text and images. Studies show top-left placement is the most effective for
brand recall."* The page then shows the horizontal lockup set top-left on A4, A5, envelope
and business card.

### Concept — PDF p.6 (printed "Page 05")

*"The logo concept is an upplifting of the old version from the company logo and we used the
D&Y letteers from the text of the old version to create the imain icon of the logo."*

### Icon — PDF p.7 (printed "Page 06"), "ICON Varaitions"

*"For Artistic look and use we designed an Icon for alghadeer logo to be used in a Varaiety
of Graphics designs for web and printed designs. This icon will be used in different ways
and colors."* Four samples are shown: blue + taupe, all blue, all taupe, and white outline.
The emblem may therefore be recoloured **within the palette** and used detached from the
wordmark.

### Rules the deck does NOT contain

There is **no clear-space rule, no minimum-size rule, and no exclusion-zone diagram**
anywhere in the 19 pages. The brief asked me to respect clear-space and minimum-size rules;
they do not exist to respect. What I applied instead is documented in §5.

## 4. Imagery, motifs and tone

**Image style — PDF p.10 (printed "Page 09").** Verbatim: *"we use the icon as a mask to
creat an artistic mask to the photos and you can use an image directly in its image ratio as
you find that usefull in some cases."* The page itself shows aerial masterplan photography
rendered **desaturated to near-greyscale** and cut by the icon's sweeping curve against the
charcoal ground. The directive is therefore twofold: photography is monochrome/low-
saturation, and the icon silhouette may be used as a photo mask.

**Iconography — PDF p.11 (printed "Page 10").** A 44-icon set, all **thin-line outline**,
uniform stroke weight, white on charcoal, on real-estate and construction subjects
(buildings, blueprints, cranes, keys, helmets, measuring tools). No filled or duotone icons
appear anywhere.

**Paragraph style — PDF p.9 (printed "Page 08").** A "TO DO & NOT TO DO" page, borrowed from
generic typesetting stock material but explicit in its marks:

- ✔ **left-aligned** body text; ✘ centred; ✘ justified.
- ✔ **soft contrast** (*"Contraste suave"*); ✘ strong contrast (*"Contraste fuerte"*).
  Read literally, this endorses charcoal-on-light-grey over black-on-white — which is what
  the `#414042` on `#F7F7F7` pairing in the proposal now does.
- F-pattern and Z-pattern reading layouts are both shown as acceptable.

**Tone of voice.** No tone-of-voice section exists. The nearest statement is PDF p.2
(printed "Page 01"): *"ALGHADEER brand communicates excellence and usually serves as the
first impression of Alghadeer with any audience in person or online."*

**Naming.** The deck is inconsistent: the cover sets the name solid as **ALGHADEER**, the
body text uses both "ALGHADEER" and "Alghadeer", and the logo lockup reads
**AL GHADEER REAL ESTATE INVESTMENT** — two words, and **singular** "INVESTMENT". No
capitalisation rule is stated. Contact details on PDF p.13 (printed "Page 12") give the
domain **alghadeerrealestate.com**.

## 5. How this was applied to the proposal

`proposals/alghadeer.html` was built on a navy-and-gold palette reverse-engineered from the
client's website and logo. None of it survives contact with the official plate: the identity
is charcoal, silver and blue, with amber and taupe in support.

### Role mapping

The CSS custom-property **names** were left alone (`--navy`, `--gold`, …). They are now role
names, not colour names — `--gold` carries the accent role and holds the CI blue. Renaming
~250 call sites would have buried the visual diff in mechanical churn with no functional
gain; a comment block at the top of `:root` records the mapping. This is a deliberate
trade-off and the one piece of tidiness deliberately left undone.

### Which accent leads

The deck ranks **blue as primary** and **amber as secondary**, and the logo is overwhelmingly
blue with the amber confined to a single arc. The proposal follows that hierarchy: blue is
the structural accent (rules, kickers, active states, links, the scale, the needle), and
amber appears only where value is being communicated on a dark ground — the sticky bill's
headline price and the benchmark scorecard's "match this build" figure. Inverting this and
using amber as a straight substitute for the old gold would have been the smaller visual
change, but it would have promoted a secondary colour over a primary one.

### Contrast

`#0082CA` on the darkest charcoal is about 4.0:1 — fine for rules, large numerals and
interactive states, marginal for small text. Small text on dark grounds therefore uses the
blue **tint** `#63B8E6` (about 8.2:1), and small text on light grounds uses the blue
**shade** `#00679F` (about 6.3:1). Both are derived from the primary, not invented.

### Logo application

With no clear-space or minimum-size rule in the deck, I applied the constraint that actually
governs: legibility.

- The nav mark (30 px) and the two product mockups keep the **silver emblem** — approved on
  dark, and the only variant that survives at that size, since the lockup's Latin wordmark
  would be sub-pixel.
- The cover replaces a typographic stand-in with the **full-colour lockup**, which is
  approved on dark and puts the client's real mark, with both its Arabic and Latin names, on
  the first screen.
- Placement already complies with the top-left rule: the nav brand sits in the upper left.
